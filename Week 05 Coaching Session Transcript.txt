======================================================================
"Cost" is how much work bcrypt does to produce one hash. It's also called the work factor or salt rounds. Bcrypt is deliberately slow, and the cost is the dial that controls how slow.

- The cost is an exponent. Bcrypt repeats its internal scrambling step about 2^cost times, so a cost of 10 means about 2^10 = 1,024 rounds.
- Every +1 doubles the work. Cost 11 takes about twice as long as 10, and cost 12 about four times as long.

Why would you want hashing to be slow?

- For a real user logging in, a hash taking something like 50-100 milliseconds is invisible. They only do it once per login.
- For an attacker with a stolen database, who has to try millions or billions of password guesses, that tiny delay adds up to months or years of computing time.

The trade-off is that if the cost is too high, your own server gets slow, especially when many people log in at once. Cost 10 is bcrypt's common default, and many apps now use 12 as computers get faster.
